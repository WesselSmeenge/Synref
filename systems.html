FOCUS
App blockers
Remove access to distraction at the source, not at the point of temptation.
01

Delete social media apps from your phone

Not disable. Delete. If you want to use them, use the browser version only. The extra friction of logging in on a browser significantly reduces impulsive use. Most people find they do not bother.

02

Install a website blocker on your computer

Use a tool that blocks distracting websites during defined work hours. The key is to set it up in advance, when your intentions are clear, not in the moment when resistance is high.

03

Define your focus hours

Decide when you will do deep work. Block those hours in your schedule. During those hours, your phone goes in another room and notifications are off. No exceptions on weekdays.

04

Replace the habit deliberately

When you feel the urge to check your phone, you need a replacement behavior ready. A two-minute walk, writing one sentence in a notebook, or drinking water. The goal is to interrupt the automatic loop with something intentional.

SLEEP
Sleep routine
A consistent wind-down system that signals your brain to rest.
01

Fix your wake time first

Choose a wake time and commit to it every day including weekends. Your circadian rhythm is anchored by your wake time, not your bedtime. Everything else follows from this one decision.

02

Start your wind-down 60 minutes before bed

At the same time each evening, dim the lights in your home. Lower light levels trigger melatonin production. This is a biological signal, not a comfort preference. It matters more than most people think.

03

No screens 30 minutes before sleep

Blue light from screens suppresses melatonin directly. If you must use a screen, use night mode and reduce brightness to the minimum. Better: put the phone in another room. Read a physical book instead.

04

Keep your bedroom cool and dark

Your core body temperature needs to drop slightly to initiate sleep. A cool room (around 18 degrees Celsius) accelerates this. Complete darkness removes light stimulation that keeps the brain alert.

05

Do a brief brain dump before bed

Write down everything on your mind for five minutes. Unfinished tasks and unresolved thoughts are a major cause of difficulty falling asleep. Writing them down signals to your brain that they are captured and do not need to be held in working memory overnight.

REFLECTION
Daily reflection system
A simple end-of-day structure to review your actions and decisions.
01

Set a fixed reflection time

Choose a time that works every day, ideally the same 10 minutes each evening. Attach it to an existing habit such as after dinner or before brushing your teeth. Consistency matters more than duration.

02

Ask three questions

What went well today and why? What did not go as intended and what caused it? What is one thing I will do differently tomorrow? Write the answers. Writing forces clarity that thinking alone does not.

03

Review weekly

Once a week, read back your daily reflections. Look for patterns. Which problems repeat? Which intentions never become actions? The weekly review turns isolated observations into usable insight.

04

Keep it short

Ten minutes maximum. The goal is not journaling for its own sake. It is extracting the signal from your day so you can act on it. Short and consistent beats long and occasional every time.
